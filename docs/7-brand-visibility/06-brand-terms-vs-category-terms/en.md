# A brand term and a category term answer two different questions

**In April 2025 the English Wikipedia page for Lululemon drew 56,057 monthly views. In February 2024 it drew 1,559 — a 39x jump in a year. Over the same stretch the category term "yoga pants" fell from 3,050 to 2,386, down 22%. The brand term rose, the category term fell, and the two lines parted ways in March 2025.**

Search engines treat "lululemon" and "yoga pants" as two kinds of query that are not interchangeable. The first is a **brand term** — a search carrying a company, product or brand name. The second is a **category term** — a generic search with no brand in it, what the trade also calls a generic keyword. Someone typing "lululemon" already knows who you are and is heading to buy; someone typing "yoga pants" may never have heard of you. Mixing both into one number hides two things at once: how much weight the brand carries in other people's heads, and how much room the category still has.

## One term rose 349% while the other two fell

Start with Q2 2025. The brand term Lululemon recorded 144,623 quarterly views, up from 32,212 in Q1 — a 349% jump in one quarter. In the same window the category term Yoga pants fell from 12,090 to 7,847, down 35%, and Athletic shoe fell from 3,679 to 3,599, down 2%.

## The two kinds of query sit at different points in the decision

**A brand term is a search that names you.** Someone who searches "lululemon" already knows the brand. They search to buy, to compare prices, to check stock. **Brand-term searchers are buyers who have not hit checkout.**

**A category term is a search with no brand name in it.** Someone searching "yoga pants" may never have heard of Lululemon. **Category-term searchers are people still learning the market.**

**Brand terms tell you about brand health: how many people have the brand in their heads.** A 349% quarterly rise while category terms fall means awareness of "Lululemon" jumped, and that push did not drag the category up with it — it happened while the category was weakening. That pattern usually comes from a burst of exposure: an event, a collaboration, a controversy, one video that broke.

**Category terms tell you about market opportunity: how many people are still buying, and whether the category is saturated.** A 35% drop is bad news for Lululemon: **it means new customers cannot find their way in.** A high brand-term number only counts existing customers and the curious.

One caveat first. This page counts English Wikipedia page views. **That is "how many people looked this word up", not "how many people typed it into a search box".** Wikipedia's audience skews toward researchers, so the absolute values are not search volume — **the direction of the trend holds**.

## The quarter when one brand term rose and the category fell

This quarter has a date on it. On March 10, 2025, Lululemon's daily pageviews jumped from 330 the day before to 843, passed 1,000 on March 16, hit 2,252 on March 28, and peaked at 3,373 on April 15. Over the same stretch Athletic shoe stayed between 25 and 60 views a day.

## Report the two kinds of terms separately

Brand terms and category terms do not combine into a single "total search volume". The moment you add them, you report two opposite things as one.

**Label the kind of term next to every number, and report both directions together.** Each figure needs to say whether it is a brand term or a category term, whether it is an absolute count or an index, and which date is the baseline. In the second chart above, the brand term is 39x and the category term is −22%; two numbers pointing in opposite directions, presented as one, is noise. "Brand terms +349%, category terms −35%" is a complete sentence; "Brand terms +349%" is half a sentence.

**When brand terms rise, be able to answer what the exposure bought.** The answer for this quarter: nothing, and operating income fell 22%. The event worked — a 300-person waitlist, and Archive's co-founder said it "had an impact on sales." But **between brand awareness and the company wallet there is a stretch of distance that people vote with their feet.** A brand-term number also has to land on revenue or operating income before it counts.

**Competitors cannot take brand-term volume away from you. Competitors can take category-term volume away from you.** A category term is a shelf everybody fights over; a brand term is your own address plate. So when category terms fall, work out whether the market shrank or the share was taken: the Yoga pants category term fell 22% in 2025 while Alo Yoga's and Vuori's brand terms both grew, which looks more like share loss. **The response to those two situations is opposite in every way**, and only pulling competitors' brand terms in alongside tells them apart.

**Brand terms are how much other people remember you. Category terms are how much room the market still has.** They get read and reported separately. **A report with only the brand-term growth is a report with its last page missing.**
