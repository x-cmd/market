# Four numbers, one brand, and only one of them moved

**On 2026-09-18, On Holding signed Kylian Mbappé. That day On's public mentions ran at 8.74 times its normal baseline, while the other 11 running-shoe brands all sat between 0.73 and 1.08. Seven weeks later, AI named On zero times across 20 football questions, and four times said Mbappé wears Nike. Mentions moved. Nothing else did.**

A brand reports on four things at once: search share, social mentions, AI mentions, direct traffic. Four units, four questions. Any one of them alone produces a confident answer that is wrong.

## Each measure answers a different question

**Search share** counts slots, not people: when someone searches this category, how many of the results are yours?

**Social mentions** count the people who already know you: how many will open your page on their own?

**AI mentions** count what a model has in mind: if you ask a model a question, do you get named?

**Direct traffic** counts memory: how many people type your address without going through search, social, or AI?

**No one of the four can substitute for another.** Merging them into a single "brand health" score adds four numbers with different units, denominators, and time spans. When the score moves, you cannot tell which line moved.

## The four lines for On Holding AG

The brand under measurement is **On Holding AG** (NYSE: ONON), the Swiss running-shoe maker. On 2026-09-18 it announced Mbappé as global brand ambassador and appointed Thierry Henry as football director.

**Direct traffic is almost always the one line you cannot get.** It lives inside the brand's own analytics tool. The other three lines anyone can look up, so they end up compared against the fourth, and the comparison produces a verdict on traffic you never measured. Leaving that cell empty beats filling in an estimate.

## Three questions to ask when the lines sit side by side

**Which one moved first.** Sequence gives causality somewhere to stand. Only social mentions can fix an order in time: it has daily granularity. Search share and AI mentions are same-day snapshots with no timeline, and direct traffic cannot be fetched. **A line with time granularity next to three lines without it invites the reader to mistake side by side for simultaneous.**

**Do the directions agree.** Social mentions rose (8.74x). Search share and AI mentions did not move. **Divergent directions are themselves the finding.** When all four rise, the category is probably rising. When one rises, the company did something.

**Is one line substituting for another.** One line up and another down can mean one took from the other, or that both rose. Nike ran at **0.96** that day: Mbappé's old club did not rise either. It dipped slightly.

July and August sat near 30,000. September spiked to 81,887, and October fell back to 36,555. **That pulse is a one-off event, not a trend line.** On's share of the 12-brand total jumped from 9.92% to 22.69%, then returned to 13.53%.

The closest official number to direct traffic is DTC channel net sales in the earnings release. Q2 2026 wholesale: 461.9 million Swiss francs, +4.8% year over year, +12.7% currency-neutral. DTC: 388.4 million Swiss francs, +26.0% year over year, +34.3% currency-neutral, or 45.7% of net sales of 850.3 million Swiss francs.

But that is **the quarter ending 2026-06-30**, and Mbappé was signed on 2026-09-18, so **it happened after the quarter closed** and the release cannot contain a trace of it. In the same release, wholesale growth came in at less than half the DTC rate, and the company attributes that to deliberately tightening wholesale sell-in. **The quarter before Mbappé was already growing mainly through its own direct channel.**

One sentence sits closer to "how loud is this brand" than any of the four numbers: **"global brand awareness rose to 30%".** That is the company's own disclosed figure, on the same yardstick every company uses, and it can only be read out of an earnings release.

## Where a single line misleads

**Read only search share** and the conclusion is "On solidly holds the top tier of running-shoe search." Not wrong. It rests on 4 slots, tied with Altra, ASICS, and HOKA. But it misses that On has already entered football while the search side shows nothing. To search, On looks like **a brand with no football business**.

**Read only social mentions** and the conclusion is "On's brand heat changed completely after 2026-09-18." Wrong. Seven weeks later, pageviews are back at the 36,555 norm, 26 views from September's monthly average of 36,529. **A peak is not a waterline.** Reading mentions alone turns one day into the new normal for a quarter.

**Read only AI mentions** and the conclusion is "On ranks 8th in AI, the opportunity is big." You see nothing of On scoring zero across the 20 football questions. **Worse, the four "Mbappé wears Nike" answers tell you two things at once**: that On ranks 8th in AI, and that AI knows nothing about On's biggest signing. Both sit in the same dataset, and reading one hides the other.

**Read only direct traffic** and the conclusion is "DTC is up 26%, the brand is healthy." Correct, and it describes **everything before 2026-06-30**. Applied to what happened after 2026-09-18, it answers a different question.

**The four together tell the whole story.** On 2026-09-18 the line that moved was social mentions, upward, by a factor of 8.74, and the peer comparison shows this was not a category-wide move. Search share and AI mentions did not move between 2026-09-18 and 2026-11-09. Direct traffic cannot be fetched, and the nearest figure (Q2 DTC +26%) predates the event.

**So the answer to "which one moved" is: social mentions moved, and they moved for three and a half days.** Anyone claiming this data shows "On has taken off on the AI side" has to name the date their numbers came from.

**One line on its own gives you that line's story. Four lines together tell you which story is true.**
