# Search rank and AI recommendation are two different scores, and they rarely agree

**Saucony's search share is 0%. Its AI share is 97.5% — named in 39 of 40 answers. On, the search leader, sits at 90% in search and 62.5% in AI.**

"Ranking first in search" and "being named in an AI answer" are two separate facts, and the gap is wide. On shows up in the top 10 of 9 category terms, but in only 25 of 40 AI answers; Saucony shows up in none of them, yet 39 of 40 AI answers name it. **The two numbers belong in separate columns.** Blend them into one "visibility" score and you are adding up two samples built on different rules.

## The search leader is the one AI mentions least

On leads on search: 9 of the 10 category terms put it in the top 10 results, at an average position of 5.1. On the AI side its share falls to 62.5% and it drops to 9th, behind ASICS and Nike. Altra is more extreme: 6 search queries at position 2, then 55% on the AI side, 10th. Its strength comes from the entry itself — "Altra Running" is a standalone Wikipedia article whose title carries the word "Running", so any retrieval touching running drags it in.

The opposite case says more. **Saucony's search share is 0%** — 10 category terms, 10 sets of top 10 results, not one appearance. **Its AI share is 97.5%.** Mizuno: 0% and 82.5%. Puma: 0% and 67.5%. Under Armour: 0% and 45%. Skechers: 0% in search, 17.5% in AI.

Search surfaces brands with their own entries — On, Altra, HOKA, Merrell, Nike. The AI top 10 are the traditional running names: New Balance, Brooks, Saucony, ASICS, HOKA, Nike, Mizuno, Adidas, Puma. Two different markets, from the same category terms.

**Search rewards being written up as an article. AI rewards being written up as a recommendation.** Whether a brand has a Wikipedia entry named after it decides whether it can reach the search side at all. Saucony's entry exists and still gets buried under more general entries. On gets pulled in because its entry, titled "On (company)", carries category meaning.

## How the search share is counted

**Search corpus.** Full-text search on English Wikipedia, because the method has to be reproducible — free, no key, one command to retrieve. Search engines cost money or throw captchas.

**Terms and depth.** 10 category terms, none containing a brand name: running shoes, best running shoes, running shoe recommendations, cushioned running shoes, carbon plate running shoes, trail running shoes, marathon running shoes, running shoes for beginners, wide toe box running shoes, minimalist running shoes. Top 10 results per term.

**Search share.** A brand's share is the share of queries whose top 10 results mention it, so 9 of 10 gives 90%. A mention means the name appears in the title, the one-line description or the body excerpt, matched on word boundaries so short names like "On" do not fire by accident. Spellings fold together: Hoka and HOKA are one brand, as are adidas and Adidas.

Under these rules **12 brands surface**: On, Altra, ASICS, Brooks, HOKA, Merrell, New Balance, Nike, Adidas, Reebok, Vibram, Vivobarefoot — **not because the category has 12 brands, but because this corpus surfaces 12.** It measures ranking inside one corpus, not ranking across search engines, so **change the search engine and this number has to be pulled again.**

## How the AI share is counted

**AI question.** The same 10 terms go to the model verbatim, one answer per term, asked 4 times each for 40 answers. The wording is fixed: `A shopper asks: "<term>". Name the running shoe brands you would list in your answer.`

**AI share.** A brand's share is the share of answers that mention it, so 39 of 40 gives 97.5%, matched on word boundaries under the same folding rules.

**Why four repetitions.** Generation is a sample, so the same question can produce different answers. Run `minimalist running shoes` and the first answer lists Vibram, INOV-8 and Reebok; the third replaces those three with HOKA, Puma and On. **A single run measures one sample, not a share.** This round surfaces **32 brands**, averaging 12.1 names per answer, against 12 on the search side.

## Why the two numbers cannot share one axis

**Keep them out of a single coordinate system.** Three things differ, and each one alone makes the comparison meaningless.

**The samples differ.** Search is 10 queries and 100 pages, where each brand fights for a slot among a dozen competitors. AI is 40 texts averaging 12 names each. **A brand at 97.5% on the AI side is in 39 of 40 answers; it cannot also claim 97.5% in a list with ten places.** Different denominators, so the percentages compare capacity, not performance.

**The time windows differ.** Both are snapshots taken at the moment of the pull: the order Wikipedia returned, and the model as it stood. **Models change with every version update.** This run used MiniMax-M3.1-Flash, answers generated on 2026-11-09. Change the model and the AI number moves; the search number does not.

**The questions differ.** On the search side you type a term and the system returns pages by relevance. On the AI side the same term goes to a model that writes a paragraph first and pulls brand names out of it — **it may read the request as "recommend", as "list", or as "explain this category".** The prompt is printed here so nobody rewords it and calls the data wrong.

**Brand names have to be fixed before the pull.** The universe of 32 was set beforehand; add a brand later and the shares get rewritten.

## One question, two sets of brands

For "trail running shoes", the top 10 search results mention 5 brands: Altra, On, HOKA, Merrell, New Balance. The same terms sent to the AI surface 22 brands across the 40 answers, adding Nike, ASICS, Adidas, Brooks, Mizuno, Salomon, Saucony, Under Armour, Topo, INOV-8, La Sportiva, Scarpa, Vibram, Columbia, Craft, Joma and Keen.

The gap is about capacity, not quality. The search top 10 is ten slots every brand fights for. An AI answer is prose, so listing 22 names costs no extra room and **almost every brand that exists can get in**. La Sportiva and Scarpa are mainstream trail shoes with no seat in the search top 10. **But being listed is not being ranked first.** The 22 brands sit on a flat list, and modifiers like "best" or "most popular" hide the order inside the prose. The share counts who was named, not who came first.

## Report the two shares apart, never as one number

**This page has two numbers, not one.** Blending them into a "visibility" score means weighting one share against the other, and the problem is not the arithmetic. It is that the method compresses two incomparable things into one: search measures Wikipedia rank, AI measures model generation. **The weight decides the answer, and the weight has no basis.** A 50/50 split and a 70/30 split reverse the order of On and Saucony entirely — On is 90% in search and 62.5% in AI, Saucony is 0% in search and 97.5% in AI. Which weight is right depends on which channel matters, and that is why these belong in separate columns.

Read across the table and the gaps carry the finding. Saucony at 0% and 97.5% has content assets but no article asset. Altra at 60% and 55% shows up on both sides but leads neither. On at 90% and 62.5% has its advantage in retrieval rank, not in generated recommendations.
