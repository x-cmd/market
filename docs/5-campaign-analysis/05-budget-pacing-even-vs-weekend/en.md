# The same budget buys 14,286 more impressions when you spread it out

**14,000 yuan, two weeks, two ways to spend it. The even plan buys 14,286 more impressions, and not one yuan extra goes out the door.**

"Pacing" means the shape you spread your budget across the calendar: 1,000 yuan a day for fourteen straight days, or 400 on weekdays and 2,500 on weekends, same fourteen days. It is not a detail on the schedule. It feeds into the auction environment, into buying intent, into whether your inventory can absorb the traffic at all.

**One line to keep: pacing is a variable in its own right, and it lands in the result the same way bid, targeting and creative do.**

## Why the shape of the spend changes the outcome

**Because a bidding system prices supply and demand right now, not your average for the month.**

Three numbers move every minute: how many advertisers want the same placement, how much inventory it has, how many people are actually ready to buy. The same budget spent on a weekday and spent on a weekend walks into two different auction environments.

**The third of those three matters most: demand is not flat either.** The UK's official statistics office (ONS) publishes one series that shows this well — internet sales as a share of total retail sales. Across 2025 the share swung between 26% and 32%: August's 26.1% was the year's low, October 28.0%, November jumped to 32.4% as the year's high, December fell back to 29.6%.

**That shape repeats every year.** November 2024 was 30.3%, November 2025 was 32.4% — the yearly high both times. August and January sit at the bottom.

**A rising online share in November means people who would have browsed in physical stores bought online instead.** That demand was not created from nothing. Black Friday-type promotions moved it off the high street, and when the promotion ends it moves back down. **Budget as if every day looks like the annual average and you will understate demand in November and overstate it in August.**

## Three shapes you can pick from

**Even: the same budget every day, traffic spread across the days.** The upside is stable daily volume and enough data for the learning algorithm to accumulate. The downside is that it assumes every day looks the same, and that is not true.

**Concentrated: most of the money lands on a handful of days.** The upside is access to peak traffic during a big promotion. The downside is that those days are the most crowded and the most expensive, and a spike in volume can outrun your inventory and your support desk.

**There is a third option, and it is the one that gets missed most: follow demand.** Not even, and not concentrated at random — align the shape of the budget to the shape of real demand.

Etsy's buyer base shows why. **Etsy Inc.'s 2025 10-K discloses 86.5 million active buyers, and the average one bought about 3 times a year.** Half of them ordered once for the whole year, and the other half bought on roughly 5 days.

**And habitual buyers — the 5.9 million who bought 6 or more times a year, about 7% of active buyers — produced roughly 40% of 2025 GMS.**

**One number in that structure connects straight to pacing: the concentration of GMS follows the distribution of purchase frequency, not a smooth purchasing population.** Half the buyers show up for a single day a year, so the slice of your budget landing on them across 365 even days is close to pure waste. And one buyer in seven produces four tenths of the transactions — the days they buy are the days worth watching.

## Three layers turn pacing into a different outcome

**Layer one, the auction environment.** On the days you concentrate, every advertiser chasing the same people spends on those same days, and the price gets pushed up. You pay the same money and buy fewer impressions. This layer is a pure accounting loss.

**Layer two, how often people actually buy.** The Etsy figure — about 3 purchases a year — says most people do not buy in a run. Their buying is scattered. **Compress your budget into two days, and if your target audience is not at peak intent on those two days, what you bought was two page views, not one order.**

**Layer three, inventory.** When volume spikes, out-of-stock items and shipping delays come with it, conversion rate falls, and the refunds and bad reviews that follow drag down your account performance. **This layer gets ignored most, because it never shows up in an ad report. It shows up in support tickets and in the refund rate.**

**All three layers make the same money land differently, and none of them sits in the "bid" column.**

## The same 14,000 yuan over two weeks, two ways

**Take one worked example.** Budget of 14,000 yuan over two weeks: 10 weekdays and 4 weekend days. Weekday CPM 60 yuan, weekend CPM 70 yuan. Weekends are more crowded, so they cost 10 yuan more.

**Plan one, even spending:** 1,000 yuan a day on weekdays, 1,000 yuan a day on weekends. The 10,000 yuan of weekdays buys 166,667 impressions, the 4,000 yuan of weekends buys 57,143, for 223,810 in total. **Blended CPM: 62.55 yuan.**

**Plan two, pushed onto weekends:** 400 yuan a day on weekdays, 2,500 yuan a day on weekends. The 4,000 yuan of weekdays buys 66,667 impressions, the 10,000 yuan of weekends buys 142,857, for 209,524 in total. **Blended CPM: 66.82 yuan.**

**Same 14,000 yuan. Even spending buys 14,286 more impressions, 6.8% more.**

**And that is only the "weekends cost more" layer.** If weekend conversion is also below weekday conversion, the gap on both sides widens. **There is no reliable public data for that layer, so no number is given here.**

**The first layer is settled though: pushing money into the more crowded hours means paying a higher unit price.**

**Concentration wins in one case: when that weekend has a promotion on it, and the promotion really does lift demand.** Then the CPM premium is worth paying. **Etsy's own 10-K spells out this pattern.** Sellers see increased sales and use more Etsy services during the fourth-quarter holiday shopping season. That pushes GMS and revenue higher in the fourth quarter of every fiscal year, and costs and marketing expenses generally move with it.

**So the question is not "even good" or "concentrated good". The question is whether you know where to push, and whether demand at the point you push actually rises.**

## Pacing is a variable, not an execution detail

**Here is a company that made money by changing the shape of its spend.** Etsy Inc.'s 2025 10-K credits three things for better ad efficiency: machine-learning ranking improvements, **more efficient seller budget pacing**, and blending in-site ads more seamlessly into organic search results.

**With all three in place, Etsy's ad revenue grew 62.2 million dollars year on year.** Total revenue rose 2.7% to 2,883.501 million dollars, and **the take rate went from 22.3% to 24.2%, a gain of 190 basis points.** Etsy said its 2025 marketplace GMS year-on-year performance improved by nearly 9 percentage points between the first and fourth quarters.

**In the first three quarters of 2026, ad revenue grew a further 64.4 million dollars year on year, and 30.9 million dollars in the third quarter alone.** Etsy attributes this to higher click volume first, and only second to a higher price per click.

**Note that order.** Click volume is the pacing question. Price is the auction question. **Pacing moves first, and price follows.**

**Three things to take off this page.**

**First, pacing is a dial you can turn, not an appendix to the schedule.** Listed advertising software company Marin Software says in its filings that the new features it is deploying include "Marin budget pacing and dynamic allocation tools". **People who spend other people's money think this is valuable enough to put it in the product name.**

**Second, measure demand before scheduling budget.** Your own backend conversion curve is broken out by day. Mark the holidays on it and look at whether those days convert high or low. **Do not spread the daily money against an annual average of demand.**

**Third, treat pacing as a variable worth testing on its own.** Same money, same bid, same targeting, only the time distribution changes. Switch every two weeks and write down what happened. **Only then do you know whether that curve is a property of your channel, or the residue of what you spent last week.**
