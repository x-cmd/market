# Spread the same budget over the calendar and you buy 14,286 more impressions

**14,000 yuan, two weeks, two ways to spend it. Spreading the money evenly buys 14,286 more impressions, 6.8% more, on an identical budget.**

Pacing means the shape you spread a budget across the calendar. The same fourteen days can be paid evenly, or pushed onto the weekends. That shape decides which auction you walk into, how many people are ready to buy, and whether your stock can carry the traffic.

## The auction prices the moment, not your monthly average

A bidding system prices supply and demand right now. Three numbers move every minute: how many advertisers want the same placement, how much inventory it has, and how many people are ready to buy. The same budget spent on a Tuesday and on a Saturday walks into two different auctions.

Demand is not flat either. The UK's official statistics office (ONS) publishes internet sales as a share of total retail sales. Across 2025 the share swung between 25.9% and 32.4%: February was the low at 25.9%, October 28.0%, November jumped to 32.4% as the high, December fell back to 29.6%.

That shape repeats every year. In 2024 the lows were February 25.9%, March 25.8% and August 25.5%, and the high came in November at 30.3%. In 2025 the high was again November, at 32.4%.

A rising online share in November means people who would have walked into physical stores bought online instead. That demand was not created from nothing: Black Friday-type promotions moved it off the high street, and when the promotion ends it slides back down. Budget as if every day looks like the annual average and you will understate November while overstating August.

## Three shapes, and the one that gets missed

**Even.** The same budget every day. Daily volume stays stable and the learning algorithm has time to accumulate data. The cost is the assumption that every day looks the same, and none of them does.

**Concentrated.** Most of the money lands on a handful of days. You get peak traffic during a promotion. The cost is that those are the most crowded and most expensive days, and a volume spike can outrun your inventory and your support desk.

**Following demand.** The option that gets skipped most. Not even, and not concentrated at random: align the shape of the budget to the shape of real demand.

Etsy's buyer base shows why. Its 2025 Form 10-K discloses 86.5 million active buyers, and the average one bought about 3 times a year. Half ordered once that year; the other half bought on roughly 5 days. Of the base, 5.9 million — the habitual buyers, who spent more than 200 dollars over the past twelve months and ordered on six or more days — account for roughly 7% and about 40% of 2025 GMS.

That concentration lands straight on pacing. Half the buyers show up for a single day a year, so budget landing on them across 365 even days is close to pure waste. The one buyer in seven who produces four tenths of the transactions, on the other hand, has purchase days worth watching.

## Three things sit between the same money and a different result

**The auction environment.** On concentrated days, every advertiser chasing the same people spends on those days too, and the price gets pushed up. You pay the same money and buy fewer impressions. This layer is pure loss.

**How often people actually buy.** About 3 purchases a year means most people do not buy in a run. Compress your budget into two days, and if your audience is not at peak intent then, what you bought was two page views, not one order.

**Inventory.** When volume spikes, out-of-stock items and shipping delays come with it. Conversion rate falls, and the refunds and bad reviews that follow drag down your account performance. This layer gets ignored most because it never shows up in an ad report. It shows up in support tickets and the refund rate.

All three change what the same money buys, and none sits in the "bid" column.

## The same 14,000 yuan over two weeks, two ways

Budget of 14,000 yuan over two weeks: 10 weekdays and 4 weekend days. Weekday CPM 60 yuan, weekend CPM 70 yuan, because weekends are more crowded and cost 10 yuan more.

| Plan | Weekday spend | Weekend spend | Impressions | Blended CPM |
|---|---|---|---|---|

That covers only the "weekends cost more" layer. If weekend conversion also runs below weekday conversion, the gap widens, but no citable public data covers that layer, so no number is given here.

Concentration wins in one case: when a promotion sits on that weekend and really does lift demand. Then the CPM premium is worth paying. Etsy's own 10-K describes the pattern. Sellers see increased sales and use more Etsy services during the fourth-quarter holiday shopping season, which pushes GMS and revenue higher in the fourth quarter of every fiscal year.

So the question is not whether even or concentrated wins. It is whether you know where to push, and whether demand there rises.

## Etsy made money from the shape of its spend

Etsy's 2025 10-K credits three things for better ad efficiency: machine-learning ranking improvements, more efficient seller budget pacing, and blending in-site ads into organic results.

With all three in place, Etsy's ad revenue grew 62.2 million dollars year on year. Total revenue rose 2.7% to 2,883.501 million dollars, and the take rate went from 22.3% to 24.2%, up 190 basis points. Etsy said its 2025 marketplace GMS year-on-year performance improved by nearly 9 percentage points from Q1 to Q4.

## Put pacing on the same shelf as bid and targeting

Listed advertising software company Marin Software says in its filings that the features it is deploying include "Marin budget pacing and dynamic allocation tools". People who spend other people's money think that is worth a product name.

Demand can be measured. Your backend conversion curve is already broken out by day. Mark the holidays on it and see whether those days convert high or low, then budget against the demand on the day and on the holiday rather than against an annual average.

Pacing can also be tested on its own. Same money, same bid, same targeting, only the time distribution changes. Switch every two weeks and write down what happened. Only then do you know whether that curve belongs to your channel, or is the residue of last week's spend.
