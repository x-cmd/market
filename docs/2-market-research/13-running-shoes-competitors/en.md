# Nike's revenue has not grown in three years. HOKA's parent doubled in five

Nike's latest fiscal year came in at 46.40 billion dollars. Three years earlier it was 51.22. Deckers Outdoor, the parent of UGG and HOKA, went from 2.55 billion in FY2021 to 5.47 billion in FY2026 — more than double, and not one down year along the way.

Running shoes feel crowded. Nike, Adidas, ASICS, Brooks, Saucony, New Balance, HOKA, On. But crowded is a feeling, not a number. Here are the numbers first, then the crowding.

## Nobody counts the running shoe market

**Running shoes have no official statistical definition.** The industry trackers publish "footwear", "apparel and footwear", "sports goods". Nothing is published as "running shoes".

So this research starts at the layer that can actually be verified: brand revenue as reported in the companies' own filings. No market-size estimate is chased, because those numbers are the softest kind. Audited revenue is the hardest.

The cost of that choice is a flaw you will keep running into: fiscal years end on different dates. The benefit is that every number traces back to an audited statement and can be pulled from the original filing.

## Read the fiscal year column before you read the bars

**The four fiscal years end on four different dates.** Nike closes at the end of May, Deckers at the end of March, Skechers at the end of December, Wolverine in early January (it runs a 52/53-week year).

That makes the sentence "Skechers at 8.97 is bigger than Deckers at 5.47" **wrong**. Skechers' 8.97 is FY2024, ending 2024-12-31. Deckers' 5.47 is FY2026, ending 2026-03-31. The two sit a year and a half apart. EDGAR has no XBRL records yet for the two fiscal years Skechers has reported since.

**Align the fiscal years before comparing across companies.** It is the cell in a competitor table that is easiest to get wrong and hardest to notice.

The lines pull apart.

**Nike**: 51.22 billion in FY2023, 46.40 in FY2026. Down about 9% over three years. It fell to 46.31 in FY2025 and came back to 46.40 in FY2026 — flat, in effect.

**Deckers** (parent of UGG and HOKA): 2.55 in FY2021, 5.47 in FY2026. **More than double in five years**, and the line never gave any of it back.

**Skechers** dropped to 4.60 in 2020 (the pandemic) and then climbed to 8.97. That rise is steeper than Deckers', but it starts from a base an order of magnitude smaller.

**Wolverine** (parent of Brooks and Saucony) is shrinking: 2.68 → 2.24 → 1.75, with a small recovery to 1.87.

So "the running shoe market" is not one number. It is four fates: an incumbent going sideways, a newcomer doubling, a contender climbing fast, an old brand retreating. **Stack all four on one axis and the story disappears. Read the four lines separately and it is obvious.**

## Shoe prices come from the CPI

The question "did running shoes get more expensive" ought to be answered with brand retail prices. But brand price lists run per model, and following one across time means maintaining the list yourself. So this research substitutes the **US footwear CPI** (BLS, seasonally adjusted, 1982-84=100). It covers all footwear, not only running shoes. What it gives up on specificity, it pays back in continuity, reproducibility and an official definition.

Put that next to the revenue lines and something shows up: shoes got sharply more expensive in 2021–2022, and over the same stretch Nike's revenue sat flat around 51 billion dollars while Deckers kept climbing. **Footwear prices rising as a category does not mean every brand raised its prices.**

## The brands you cannot pull

Four brands on this page have no number, and the reason matters:

- **Adidas** (Germany), **ASICS** (Japan), **New Balance** (unlisted) — not in the US SEC XBRL database, so there is no machine-readable revenue to pull
- **On Holding** (Switzerland, code 1858985) — files on EDGAR, but with IFRS tags, so nothing comes back under `us-gaap`

**These four cells stay empty. No estimated figures are filled in.** Getting the numbers means reading each company's annual report by hand. That is a slower road, and it cannot be reproduced with a single command.

The empty cells carry information too: **whether a brand's numbers are machine-readable in public data is itself a real barrier in competitor research.**

## Align the years, and let the gaps stay gaps

The filings describe four fates at once. Nike has not grown in three years (FY2023 51.22 → FY2026 46.40). Deckers more than doubled in five (2.55 → 5.47). Skechers climbed fast but sits an order of magnitude smaller. Wolverine is retreating.

**Align the fiscal years before comparing.** Skechers' 8.97 is FY2024; Deckers' 5.47 is FY2026. Set them side by side without that adjustment and the conclusion flips.

For price, the footwear CPI stands in: +6% over the decade. Four cells — Adidas, ASICS, New Balance, On — stay empty, because those brands are not in the SEC XBRL database.
